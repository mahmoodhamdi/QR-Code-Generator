When most people think "QR menu" they picture a sticker on the table with a link to a PDF. That's the lazy version. Below are five ways forward-thinking restaurants use QR codes to cut costs, boost ticket size, and keep more of their staff's attention on the people instead of the menu.

## 1. Per-table identification

Print a unique QR for each table. The code carries `?table=N` in the URL, so when a customer scans it, your order system knows which table they're at. Combined with a basic ordering form, you've replaced the waiter's order-taking pass without losing the ability to greet the customer.

**How to set this up with MenuQR Pro:** use the bulk generator, paste tables 1 through 50, and download a ZIP of personalised codes — each as a print-ready PDF you can hand to your sign-maker.

## 2. Multi-language menus without reprinting

Tourists scan a different QR than locals. Use the bulk feature to generate two QRs from the same menu URL: one ends in `?lang=en`, the other in `?lang=ar`. On the menu PDF, host both language versions and switch based on the parameter.

Saves money on translating + laminating physical menus, and you can edit prices on both at once.

## 3. Specials and seasonal menus

A static printed menu can't promote tonight's special. But a QR that points to your menu URL with a "specials" anchor can. Update the special on your menu page; every existing QR shows the new pin immediately.

This is the same QR — you don't reprint anything. Just push to your menu hosting (Google Drive, your website, whatever).

## 4. WiFi auto-join + menu in one tap

Most cafés print the WiFi password on a chalkboard. Customers squint, fail to type it, and ask staff. Instead:

- One QR shares the WiFi credentials (auto-joins).
- A second QR opens the menu.

Customers scan both in 5 seconds and your staff's time goes to actually serving food.

MenuQR Pro generates both kinds of codes side-by-side and lets you export them as a single A4 table tent.

## 5. Loyalty + feedback in the same flow

Add `?from=qr-table-7` to your menu URL. Now, when the customer finishes their meal and you ask for feedback or to join your loyalty program, you know:

- Which table they were at (cross-reference with table service).
- That they actually used the QR (vs. paper menu).
- Which campaign they came in on (if you use the URL parameter as a campaign tag).

Marketers call this attribution. For a restaurant, it's the difference between "we feel like Tuesdays are slow" and "Tuesdays at table 5 actually have the highest spend per cover."
