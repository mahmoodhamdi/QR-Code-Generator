I haven't carried paper business cards in 18 months. Nobody has noticed — except in a positive way. Below is the etiquette I've worked out for digital business cards, the pitfalls to avoid, and why it actually makes you look more, not less, professional.

## When digital wins outright

- **Tech conferences**: the host probably had a contactless registration. Showing up with paper feels two iterations behind.
- **Coffee meetings**: handing someone a printed card after a 45-minute conversation is awkward. Showing them a QR on your phone takes 3 seconds.
- **Networking events where you forgot your cards**: every single time this has happened to me, the person had a QR ready and I felt like I'd just shown up unprepared.

## When paper still wins

- **Senior executives who explicitly value the physical exchange**. Some people see the card-handing ritual as a gesture of respect. Read the room — if you're meeting a 60-year-old chairman in a traditional industry, bring paper.
- **Trade shows where the booth is the connection.** Paper cards stack on a stand and the booth manager processes them later. A QR is harder to log if everyone scans.
- **International meetings where business-card culture is strong.** Japan, parts of the Middle East — bring paper as a courtesy.

## The mistake everyone makes the first time

People think the QR opens a webpage. **It shouldn't.** A webpage means the recipient has to:

1. Scan
2. Open browser
3. Read
4. Manually save your number, name, email

That's 4 steps. They give up at step 3.

**A proper digital card is a vCard.** Scanning it opens the contacts app and offers a one-tap "Add to contacts." 1 step. They're not even thinking; they're tapping yes.

## What to put on it

In order of importance:

1. **Your photo.** People forget faces; their contacts app shouldn't.
2. **Mobile number.** The first thing they'll need.
3. **Email.** Specifically your "I check this every day" address.
4. **LinkedIn handle.** More relevant than 90% of business contexts.
5. **Job title + company.**
6. **Other social profiles** if relevant (GitHub for engineers, Twitter for journalists, Instagram for designers).
7. **Address** rarely matters — leave it out unless you run a physical business.

## The handoff

Show the QR full-screen on your phone. The recipient scans with their camera. Done.

**Don't AirDrop a vCard.** It's clunky and Android users get nothing.

**Don't text them your contact info.** That's two SMS messages and they still have to type to save you.

**Don't email them after.** Adds friction to a meeting that was already over.
